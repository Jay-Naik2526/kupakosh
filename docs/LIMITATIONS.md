# Limitations (read before quoting any number)

1. **Stand-in data.** Well-level data is public data from Norway, the USA, the UK, the Netherlands, Australia, New Zealand and Canada. Indian data is public too: NDR/DGH summaries, OISD alerts, CAG audits, PIB releases and court records. **No Oil India well data** is used or claimed.
2. **Summaries, not diaries.** Most report text is a well history, a completion report or an incident summary. These under-record problems. A Kupakosh rate is a rate of *recorded* problems; "no record" does not mean "no problem".
3. **The hazard model has no measured skill.** In leave-one-well-out testing, the model's Brier score equals the base rate's (n = 41,848). The UI therefore shows ranges and evidence counts, says "insufficient evidence" when n_eff < 3, and never claims to predict.
4. **Extraction is rule-based.**
   - Precision and recall are both 0.92 (n = 38).
   - 1,851 of 2,623 events fall below the confidence threshold and wait in the review queue.
   - The LLM pass is built but off (no key).
5. **Gold labels are AI-made.** An independent AI second pass agreed 96–100 %, but a person must verify them before any external claim.
6. **The copilot is extractive,** and its eval questions are templated, so 1.00 is optimistic.
7. **Headers only for UK, NZ and Canada.** Their report archives need a login, so no events come from them.
8. **OCR quality varies.** Clean scans read at about 91–95 % confidence, degraded ones at 30–58 %. Event confidence is scaled by OCR confidence, but garbled text can still produce a wrong event, which then waits in the review queue.
9. **US incident records.**
   - They are linked to a single well only by API number or a unique lease + block. Otherwise they go to a block aggregate, or stay unlinked (2,897 records).
   - Block aggregates are locations, not wells, and are never counted as wells.
   - Some BSEE borehole columns were inferred, because the official layout page is not machine-readable.
10. **Depths are MD.** Most exploration wells have no public trajectory here, so the mud window and correlation are approximate for deviated wells. FORGE wells have full surveys.
11. **The live look-ahead is a replay** of recorded FORGE sensor data. There is no WITSML/eRTMAC adapter.
12. **Target stack not verified.** It runs on SQLite. PostGIS, pgvector, Alembic and Docker are described but not verified on the build machine.
13. **Reviewer names are demo placeholders** (R. Das, A. Sharma) from `config/default.yaml`. Wiki approvals so far are AI approvals (item 19), not engineer approvals.
14. **Not built:**
    - Volve DDR XML ingest (licence access);
    - "Send for review" on the brief;
    - Dutch-language text rules (English patterns only);
    - Tesseract refuses a few extremely long log-strip pages, so their own text is kept.
15. **Hindsight: what the forewarned number means.**
    - Headline measures (live, 29 Sept 2026, after the leak fix and the AI review in item 18; 304 wells, 449 real problems, 36,776 layer cells): ranking accuracy AUC 0.86 (field average 0.69); problem layer flagged before the bit arrived 61.7 / 70.2 / 79.5 / 84.2 % at 6 / 10 / 15 / 20 alerts per well; right hazard in the layer's top 3: 74.4 % (random 37.5 %). Exact hazard at about 6 alerts per well: 193/449 (43 %), median 348 m ahead; the field average with the same alerts gets 86/449. The older figures below (438 problems) are kept as history.
    - AUC is flattered by the many easy layers with no problem at all; "79.5 % of problem layers flagged" costs 15 alerts per well. Both are shown with their trade-off.
    - **Fixed 28 Sept 2026:** the field base rate (the prior) used to include the tested well itself, a small leak in every earlier Hindsight number and in the leave-one-well-out Brier. The tested well and its sidetracks are now excluded; the numbers above are after the fix.
    - The ranker is trained on other wells only (grouped cross-validation); live mode uses the well's own reports only above the alert point. It assumes the planned formation column equals the recorded one, which flatters pre-drill results slightly.
    - Precision per alert is low (about 10 %), and FORGE (4 wells) shows no measured lift on its own.
    - Strict alerts (posterior ≥ 40 %) forewarn only 15 of 438 real problems (3.4 %). Their 73× lift comes from just 14 alerts, mostly in the small FORGE set, and a field-average baseline already reaches 66×.
    - The blind top-5 watch-list per well holds 137/438 (31 %, 4.4× chance), but ranking by the field-wide layer rate alone does about as well (133/438): nearby-well weighting adds little on this data.
    - 44 problems sit in layers missing from their well's formation column and can never be listed.
    - The stricter "elevated" rule flags nothing yet. Testable wells are Norway and USA only.
16. **India Analogs** use lithology and depth analogues from outside India. They are not Indian well records, and the fixes shown are scoped per well, not per interval.
17. **3D view assumptions.** Wells without a survey are drawn vertical (labelled "assumed vertical"). Positions are projected locally, which is valid within about 50 km.
18. **AI-reviewed events (28 Sept 2026).** The 1,851 low-confidence events in the review queue were reviewed by an AI pass (8 AI reviewers reading each verbatim sentence; brief in `docs/AI_REVIEW.md`), at the project lead's explicit request because no engineer was available. Result: 993 confirmed, 86 relabelled, 772 rejected; 20 depths added only when quoted verbatim from the sentence, 36 unverifiable auto-depths cleared. These events carry `method = ai_review` (reviewer "AI review, authorised by Jay Naik") and are **not** engineer-verified. Afterwards: 1,851 trusted events (1,079 AI-reviewed); Hindsight AUC 0.86, problem layer flagged ahead 79.5 % at 15 alerts per well and 84.2 % at 20; extraction recall on the AI-made gold set fell from 0.92 to 0.87 because some rule events the gold set counts were rejected.
19. **AI-approved wiki pages (29 Sept 2026).** At the project lead's explicit request (no engineer available), all 951 wiki pages were approved by `backend/scripts/approve_wiki_ai.py`. It re-ran the compiler's citation check on every page (every sentence must carry a citation; the compiler had already rejected unsupported numbers) and required at least one cited source; all 950 pending pages passed. Each approval is a normal noting para and git commit with reviewer "AI review, authorised by Jay Naik" and role "AI reviewer (not engineer-verified)". This checks form (citations present), not engineering judgement. A page whose content changes on a recompile goes back to draft.
20. **Volve daily drilling reports (30 Sept 2026).** 1,759 real daily reports from 23 Volve wellbores (Equinor release, republished on HuggingFace as `bengsoon/volve_alpaca`, CC-BY-2.0) were added as 23,447 timed activities.
    - Extraction on these reports found 654 problem events (269 flagged for review). Precision on a random sample of 40 trusted events is **0.75** (AI-labelled, pending human check; `data/eval/volve_ddr_events_check.csv`). The main remaining false hits are planned operations (setting plugs, completion and P&A steps) and normal torque figures.
    - Sodir publishes formation tops only for Volve's exploration wells (15/9-19 S, A, B), not the development "F" wells. For those, a formation is used only where the report line itself names it (86 activities); otherwise it is left unknown, never taken from a neighbouring well.
    - With these events the Hindsight test grew from 449 to 578 real problems on 306 wells. The headline moved: AUC 0.86 → **0.84**; problem layer flagged at 15 alerts per well 79.5 % → **78.4 %**; right hazard in top 3 74.4 % → **68.2 %**; exact hazard forewarned **222/578** (field average 113/578), median **394 m** ahead. These replace the figures in item 15.
    - Fixed the same day: the per-well blind view stored the learned alert flags for only one layer (a loop-indentation bug), so every well's view showed no alerts although the summary counted them. The headline numbers were never affected.
21. **Accuracy round (1 Oct 2026).** Every change below was measured on data it was *not* tuned on.
    - **Volve event precision.** The first check (0.75, n=40) had been judged on only the first 120 characters of each line. Re-judged on the full line it was 0.85 (33/40 after one more correction). The rules were then improved in rounds, and each round was measured on a **fresh random sample never looked at before**: 0.83 (50/60), then 0.78 (47/60). The remaining errors were planned intervention work (plug and abandonment, completion, wireline), so events on such lines now go to human review unless the line also states a clear problem (`extract.planned_intervention` / `strong_problem` in `config/default.yaml`). Final fresh sample: **0.88 (53/60)**, Wilson 95 % range about 0.78–0.94. Cost: trusted Volve events fell from 356 to 312; the others stay in the review queue. Sodir events were not re-extracted (the new rules would only drop two stuck-wireline-tool sentences there).
    - **Hindsight.** The learned ranker now chooses per training fold, by an inner grouped cross-validation, between one tree model, an average of three, and that average plus layer-context features (the blind estimate of the same hazard in the layer above and below). Nested, so test wells never pick the model. On 571 problems / 306 wells: AUC 0.84 → **0.85** (0.828–0.874; blind pre-drill 0.83); problem layer flagged at 15 alerts per well 78.4 % → **82.7 %**; right hazard in top 3 68.2 % → **76.9 %**; exact hazard forewarned at about 6 alerts per well 222/578 → **269/571** (47 %), median 383 m ahead. The problem count fell from 578 to 571 because the review rule above removed planned-work events. About 15 model variants were compared on the same cross-validation before the nested design was fixed; the nested numbers are the ones reported.
    - **Episode outcomes.** The old 0.68 accuracy / 0.78 precision was measured on `episodes_gold.csv`, which had also been used to tune the rules. On fresh random samples the true figure before this round was **0.44 accuracy (11/25), 0.50 precision (4/8)**. After generic fixes (plain resolution phrases, plug-back / sidetrack, the earliest-hazard-mention bug), the final blind sample, labelled before the predictions were looked at, gives **0.55 accuracy (17/31), 0.57 precision (8/14)**. Rule-based outcome reading is the weakest part of the system; an LLM pass (not configured in this build) is the obvious next step. About 23 % of sampled episodes were not drilling episodes at all (court text about the Baghjan-5 blowout, incident tables, mud-report tables).
    - **Tried and not used: "drilling déjà vu".** Matching the live sensor pattern to the hour before past problems (FORGE 16A/16B, 15 timed problems) ranked the real pre-problem hour at the 28th percentile (chance: 50th), 1 of 15 in the top 10 %. Problem times come from report lines 0.5–10 h long, far too coarse. Kept on the Accuracy page as a negative result.
    - **Engineer feedback** ("problem happened" on an alert) feeds base rates and posteriors at once, but the Hindsight test scores only on written records, so feedback cannot raise the measured accuracy. A full rebuild (`bootstrap`) drops stored feedback.
    - All labels in this round are AI-made (full lines read), pending human verification.
22. **Accuracy round 2 (1 Oct 2026).**
    - **Volve event precision 0.92 (55/60)** on a fifth fresh random sample, after excluding planned set checks, wear-bushing tools, caliper readings and negations ("none left in hole"). Remaining errors: surface losses (shakers, a riser leak) and planned plug work. Trusted Volve events: 299.
    - **Episode outcomes:** a "progress" rule for timed daily reports (a later line shows drilling or tripping on to a depth without mentioning the problem again → resolved) plus generic "cleared" phrases. Fifth fresh blind sample (50 episodes, labelled before the predictions were seen): **precision 0.75 (15/20)**, accuracy still **0.55 (22/40)**. 20 % of sampled episodes were not drilling episodes (tables, legal text).
    - **Hindsight** recomputed on the cleaner labels (570 problems): AUC 0.853 → **0.861**; forewarned 269 → **278**; layers flagged at 10 alerts per well 70.6 % → **75.8 %**; but at 15 alerts per well 82.7 % → **81.9 %** and top 3 76.9 % → **75.6 %**. Both directions reported.
    - **Tried and not used:** a local language model (Qwen2.5 7B through Ollama, offline) reading episode outcomes: 14/31 on the blind set alone (rules 17/31), 16/31 as a gap filler. A Hindsight feature from offset wells' untrusted problem mentions: within noise (AUC +0.004, layers flagged −1.6 points). The model path is kept, switched off (`episodes.run(llm=True)`).
    - **"It had happened before"** (repeats): a repeat shows a warning existed in an older report; it does not prove the problem was preventable. Records under-report, so the true share is probably higher.
    - **Round 6 (episodes):** outcome phrases that settle the problem anywhere in its own sentence ("Pumping LCM cured the static losses", "temporarily stuck"; failure phrases such as "could not be freed" checked first). Sixth fresh blind sample (50 episodes): **accuracy 0.58 (22/38)**, precision **0.75 (12/16)**. The gain over 0.55 is within the noise of a sample this size; rule-based outcome reading has reached its ceiling. After the later NLOG table/form filter sent 3 of the sample's Dutch problem lines to review, they are no longer episodes; on the 36 that remain the same rules score **0.61 (22/36)**, precision unchanged at 0.75. The Accuracy page recounts this from the labelled rows ("Verify"), which is how the drift was found.
    - **Round 6 (Volve):** surface losses (shakers, riser leaks) and small losses while setting planned plugs excluded. The sixth sample used every trusted Volve event no earlier round had seen (41): **0.90 (37/41)**. Rounds 5 and 6 together: 92/101 = 0.91. The remaining errors are one-off planned-work wordings; excluding each one would tune the rules to the sample, so this is where the rule-based precision honestly sits.
    - **Final recompute (1 Oct, after round 6):** 565 problems on 306 wells; AUC **0.861** (0.836–0.887); forewarned **276/565**, median 384 m; problem layer flagged 65.3 / 74.7 / **82.5** / 86.6 % at 6 / 10 / 15 / 20 alerts per well; right hazard in top 3 **74.9 %**; repeats 332 of 661.
    - **Fixed (1 Oct):** a test run of the Hindsight suite wrote its 80-well sample over the full result the website serves, and a server holding older events kept recomputing against a newer file. Sample runs now write their own files, writes are atomic, and the repeats card is cached on disk.
23. **More Dutch data (2 Oct 2026).** 646 more public NLOG well reports (1.5 GB, Dutch government open data) were downloaded and read: 513 new report documents on 513 wells (132 duplicate files, 1 unreadable). Scanned reports were OCR'd for at most their first 120 pages each (10,467 OCR pages).
    - **Extraction quality:** the first fresh check on the new events was only 0.67 (40/60): tables and forms in the OCR'd reports matched hazard words. A table/form filter (`extract.table_text`) now sends such lines to review. Second fresh sample: **0.88 (53/60)**. Remaining errors: wireline / logging tools stuck (not the drill string), a future plan, a lithology list.
    - **Two bugs found and fixed:** (1) the 40 most similar offsets were taken before dropping wells without reports, so in dense Dutch fields almost no documented offset survived; the hazard model, look-ahead, Hindsight and repeats now search documented wells only. (2) Wells with no recorded parent wellbore were compared None == None, dropping every such offset. NLOG publishes no parent link, so families are derived from the official names ("…-SIDETRACK1", "104A" → "104"; 996 families), and the blind test hides the whole family.
    - **Hindsight on 431 wells (306 Norway, 121 Netherlands, 4 USA), 1,015 problems:** AUC **0.855** (0.834–0.873; blind pre-drill 0.842, up from 0.835); exact hazard forewarned **500 / 1,015 (49 %)**, median 405 m, field average 299. Lower than on the old set: problem layer flagged at 15 alerts/well 82.5 % → **76.9 %**, right hazard in top 3 74.9 % → **70.2 %**. The test is twice as large, in two countries; the stronger field-average baseline (0.71) shows the Dutch records are richer, so beating it is harder. Netherlands alone: 250 / 452 forewarned (55 %), 10.8× lift.
    - **One model per region (same day).** Training one model on the Norwegian and Dutch wells together fitted both worse (the numbers above). Each region now has its own model and alert thresholds, trained and tested blind on its own wells only (`hindsight.learned.regions`; regions under 30 testable wells join the largest). This design was chosen after seeing the mixed result; it is how a deployment would be calibrated (Oil India on its own basin). Result on the same 431 wells / 1,015 problems: AUC **0.871** (0.851–0.890); Norway + USA 0.866, Netherlands **0.898**; exact hazard forewarned **579 / 1,015 (57 %)**, median 391 m (field average 313); problem layer flagged at 15 alerts/well 79.1 % overall, **82.6 %** Norway + USA (87.0 % at 20), 74.8 % Netherlands; right hazard in top 3 72.4 % overall, **77.8 %** Norway + USA, 65.7 % Netherlands.
24. **Live feed, Verify, server reliability (3 Oct 2026).**
    - **Live feed.**
      - The WITSML 1.4.1 and WITS0 readers are tested on documents we generate and on hand-written samples with foreign units.
      - They have not been tested against a real eRTMAC or a vendor WITSML server: mnemonic names differ between vendors and may need adding to `config/default.yaml → livefeed.witsml_mnemonics`.
      - Mud weight is not read from the live feed: it comes from the daily reports' mud checks.
      - Feed state is kept in memory. After a restart, senders simply keep posting.
      - The demo feeder re-sends recorded Utah FORGE data and is labelled that way.
    - **Verify.**
      - The recount uses the labelled CSV files in `data/eval/` and the current database.
      - Labels are still AI-made, pending human verification.
      - Copilot figures are listed, not re-asked, from the page.
    - **Reliability.**
      - A health check every minute restarts the site after 3 failed checks in a row.
      - A daily, integrity-checked database backup keeps the newest 7.
      - Both run on the same server unless `KK_BACKUP_S3` is set: a lost server loses its backups too.
      - `scripts/deploy_aws.sh` installs both, and keeps the previous database as `kupakosh.db.prev` when it uploads a new one.
